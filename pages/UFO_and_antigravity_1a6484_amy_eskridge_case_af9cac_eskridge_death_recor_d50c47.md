## Introduction

The public record surrounding [Amy Eskridge]({{ 'amy-eskridge/' | relative_url }})’s death is unusually narrow compared with the amount of speculation that now surrounds it. Within the broader narrative about alleged suspicious deaths among [UFO- and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }})-related researchers, the central fact is that publicly available information about Eskridge’s death is limited. Obituaries establish that she died on 11 June 2022 in Huntsville, Alabama, at age 34. Later media reporting stated that authorities treated the death as a suicide involving a self-inflicted gunshot wound. ## Why the Documentation Gap Became Part of the Story

Eskridge’s case unfolded differently because her public identity was tied to antigravity research, exotic science, [aerospace]({{ 'aerospace/' | relative_url }}) culture, and discussions that overlap with UFO-related communities.

When later reports connected her name to broader lists of deceased or missing scientists, the absence of publicly accessible investigative records became a focal point.
