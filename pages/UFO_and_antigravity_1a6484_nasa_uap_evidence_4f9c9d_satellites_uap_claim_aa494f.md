## Introduction

Can satellites settle UFO sightings? In most cases, no. One of the clearest messages emerging from NASA’s 2023 UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena) study is that public expectations often exceed what satellites can realistically do. Many people assume that NASA satellites or commercial imaging constellations continuously record every part of Earth at high resolution and could therefore confirm or refute unusual aerial sightings. In practice, satellite systems are designed for specific scientific, environmental, or commercial purposes, and most are poorly suited to capturing a small, fast-moving, unexpected object at exactly the right place and time. NASA’s evidence standard emphasises understanding what a sensor can and cannot measure before drawing conclusions from its absence or presence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s fleet of Earth-observing satellites collect the most data within the. ## Why NASA Satellites Often Cannot See Small UAP Directly

NASA operates some of the most sophisticated Earth-observing systems ever built, but they are generally optimised for studying weather, oceans, land surfaces, climate processes, and large-scale environmental changes. They are not designed as continuously recording aerial-surveillance cameras. NASA’s UAP study explicitly noted that Earth-observing satellites typically lack the spatial resolution needed to detect relatively small objects such as reported UAP.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s fleet of Earth-observing satellites collect the most data within the. Earth system, yet t...</span></span></span>

Several practical limitations explain why:

* **Resolution limits:** Many Earth-observation instruments are intended to measure environmental features spread across tens or hundreds of metres. A small aircraft-sized object may occupy only a tiny fraction of a pixel or be invisible altogether.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s fleet of Earth-observing satellites collect the most data within the. Earth system, yet t...</span></span></span>
* **Timing constraints:** Satellites do not usually stare continuously at the same location. Most revisit a given area according to orbital schedules. A sighting lasting seconds or minutes may occur entirely between satellite passes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s fleet of Earth-observing satellites collect the most data within the. Earth system, yet t...</span></span></span>
* **Field-of-view trade-offs:** Sensors designed to cover vast regions sacrifice detail. Systems optimised for global weather monitoring are fundamentally different from narrow-field reconnaissance platforms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s fleet of Earth-observing satellites collect the most data within the. Earth system, yet t...</span></span></span>
* **Unexpected targets:** UAP reports are generally unplanned events. Satellites are rarely positioned and configured specifically to observe the reported location when the event occurs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP9 Jun 2022 — The study will focus on identifying available data, how best to collect future data, and how NASA can use tha...</span></span></span>

Research in remote sensing illustrates the challenge. Even detecting ordinary vehicles, ships, or aircraft in satellite imagery remains technically difficult because such targets may occupy only a handful of pixels and must be distinguished from noise, motion artefacts, clouds, shadows, and background clutter. Academic work on satellite-video tracking repeatedly identifies “tiny object” detection as a major problem rather than a solved capability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-problems-of-tracking-objects-in-satellite-videos-a-small-objects-b-f-complex_fig1_363100074" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Figure 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The problems of tracking objects in satellite videosObject size is smaller: Due to the low spatial resolution of satellite video, moving...</span></span></span>

This creates a gap between popular assumptions and operational reality. A dramatic sighting observed from the ground may appear visually obvious to witnesses while remaining effectively invisible to many orbital sensors.

## How Commercial Imagery Might Help in Rare Cases

Commercial satellite imagery is often discussed as an alternative to government or NASA data. Modern commercial providers operate large constellations capable of producing imagery at substantially higher spatial resolution than many scientific Earth-observing missions. In theory, this improves the chances of detecting unusual aerial objects. In practice, the opportunity remains limited.

Commercial systems may help when several conditions align:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A sighting occurs over an area being imaged at that moment.
2. The object is large enough to appear distinctly in the imagery.
3. Atmospheric conditions permit a clear view.
4. Accurate time and location information exist.
5. The object remains visible long enough to be captured.

</div>

These requirements are more demanding than they first appear. Even when commercial satellites image a region, many products consist of brief snapshots rather than continuous video. Moreover, aerial targets may move significantly during image acquisition, creating ambiguity about shape, location, or apparent motion. Research examining commercial satellite archives has shown that moving vehicles and aircraft can sometimes be detected, but extracting reliable motion information requires specialised analysis and detailed knowledge of sensor behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Detection of Moving Objects in Earth Observation Satellite Images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Detection of Moving Objects in Earth Observation Satellite ImagesMay 18, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 18, 2024</span></span></span>

Commercial imagery therefore functions less as an all-seeing record and more as an occasional supplementary source. A positive detection could be valuable, but the absence of a detection usually has limited evidential value because the object may simply have been too small, too brief, or outside the imaging geometry.

## Where Satellite Data Can Still Test Sighting Claims

NASA’s study places greatest emphasis on a different use of satellite information: environmental reconstruction. Rather than asking whether a satellite photographed a UAP directly, investigators can ask what satellites reveal about the surrounding conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/NASA_Unidentified_Anomalous_Phenomena%3A_Independent_Study_Team_Report/Responses_to_Statement_of_Task" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Responses to Statement of Task</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Independent...Oct 14, 2023 — NASA&#x27;s fleet of earth-observing satellites should play a p...</span></span></span>

This approach can be surprisingly powerful.

## The Multi-Sensor Standard NASA Prefers

A recurring theme in the NASA UAP study is that no single sensor should carry the full burden of proof. Many famous UAP cases rely on isolated videos, photographs, or eyewitness accounts. ## The Most Common Misunderstanding

The most persistent misunderstanding in public UAP debates is the belief that satellites provide a complete visual record of events occurring on Earth. NASA’s assessment points in the opposite direction. Earth-observing satellites are valuable scientific instruments, but they are not omniscient observers continuously recording every object in the atmosphere.
