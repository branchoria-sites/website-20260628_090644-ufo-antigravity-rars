## Introduction

In discussions about UFO research, alleged antigravity projects and the deaths or disappearances of scientists, secrecy surrounding major national-security laboratories is often treated as evidence that extraordinary programmes must be hidden behind closed doors. A key fact is frequently overlooked: some of that secrecy is not discretionary at all. In the United States, nuclear-weapons information is governed by a specialised legal regime created by the Atomic Energy Act, which operates differently from ordinary government classification systems. Information connected to nuclear weapons can fall into the category known as Restricted Data, a status that is legally distinct from standard national-security secrets and is protected under rules that were designed specifically to prevent the spread of nuclear-weapons knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.directives.doe.gov/terms_definitions/restricted-data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: directives.doe.gov">[doe.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">directives.doe.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">restricted data</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DataRestricted Data. Understanding these rules matters because they help explain why records from nuclear laboratories can remain inaccessible, heavily redacted or difficult to discuss publicly even when there is no evidence of hidden UFO or antigravity programmes. The legal architecture itself can create an appearance of mystery that outsiders may interpret as proof of something more extraordinary than the available evidence supports.

## What Restricted Data Means in Plain Language

The Atomic Energy Act created a category called **Restricted Data (RD)** covering information about nuclear weapons design, manufacture and use, the production of special nuclear materials such as plutonium and highly enriched uranium, and certain aspects of nuclear energy production. Unlike most classified information, Restricted Data is defined directly in statute rather than solely through executive orders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.directives.doe.gov/terms_definitions/restricted-data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: directives.doe.gov">[doe.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">directives.doe.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">restricted data</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DataRestricted Data. The unusual feature is that nuclear information is commonly described as being **“born classified”** or **“born secret.”** In practical terms, this means that if information falls within the statutory definition of Restricted Data, it is treated as classified by law even if no official has formally stamped it Secret or Top Secret. The legal protection flows from the subject matter itself rather than from a separate act of classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/library/quist2/chap_3.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[fas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecy Chapter 3</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CLASSIFICATION OF INFORMATIONThe authority for classifying information as RD or FRD comes from the Atomic Energy Act of 1954. This principle developed from post-war concerns that nuclear-weapons knowledge was uniquely dangerous and could not be protected adequately through ordinary secrecy mechanisms. As a result, the law assumes that certain categories of nuclear information require protection unless and until the government deliberately removes them from that status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_Data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Restricted Data</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 3, 2025 — Restricted Data (RD) is a category of classified information in the United States that is defined by the Atomic Energy...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 3, 2025</span></span></span>

For researchers, archivists and historians, this creates a very different environment from most government records. Documents may remain restricted not because they reveal a current covert operation but because they contain information that still falls within a legally protected nuclear category. ## How Nuclear Classification Differs from Ordinary Secrets

Most classified government information in the United States falls under the category of **National Security Information (NSI)**. Under that system, an authorised official makes a conscious decision that disclosure could damage national security and formally classifies the information. Executive orders establish the rules for classification, handling and declassification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.energy.gov/ehss/statutes-regulations-and-directives-classification-program" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: energy.gov">[The Department of Energy&#x27;s Energy.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">energy.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">statutes regulations and directives classification program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Department of Energy&#x27;s Energy.govStatutes, Regulations, and Directives for Classification...Atomic Energy Act of 1954 - Establishes...</span></span></span>

Several distinctions are especially important:

* **Different legal authority:** Restricted Data derives from the Atomic Energy Act, while ordinary national-security information derives primarily from executive orders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.energy.gov/ehss/statutes-regulations-and-directives-classification-program" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: energy.gov">[The Department of Energy&#x27;s Energy.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">energy.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">statutes regulations and directives classification program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Department of Energy&#x27;s Energy.govStatutes, Regulations, and Directives for Classification...Atomic Energy Act of 1954 - Establishes...</span></span></span>
* **Born-classified status:** Restricted Data can exist without any original classification decision, whereas ordinary national-security information requires an affirmative classification action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/library/quist2/chap_3.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecy Chapter 3</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CLASSIFICATION OF INFORMATIONThe authority for classifying information as RD or FRD comes from the Atomic Energy Act of 1954. * **Different declassification process:** Only designated Department of Energy officials can declassify Restricted Data, and some categories require coordination with other agencies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/1260.28" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information InstituteWho is responsible for declassifying Restricted Data, Formerly...Only designated officials within the Departm...</span></span></span>
* **Separate marking system:** A document may be marked Secret or Top Secret while simultaneously carrying Restricted Data designations. The RD designation describes the nature of the information, not simply its sensitivity level.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_Data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Restricted Data</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 3, 2025 — Restricted Data (RD) is a category of classified information in the United States that is defined by the Atomic Energy...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 3, 2025</span></span></span>

The system also includes **Formerly Restricted Data (FRD)**, a category created when information is removed from Restricted Data because it primarily concerns the military use of nuclear weapons and can be protected under broader national-security classification rules. Even after this transfer, the information remains classified. FAS Project on Government Secrecy+2Energy.gov<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/doe/rdfrdhtm.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[sgp.fas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyProtecting the Nation&#x27;s Nuclear InformationF ormerly Restricted Data is defined by the Atomic Energy Act...</span></span></span>

This layered structure is one reason outsiders often find nuclear secrecy unusually difficult to navigate. Records may be withheld under authorities that are unfamiliar even to people who understand conventional government classification.

## Why Nuclear Laboratories Often Appear More Secretive Than Other Facilities

National-security laboratories associated with nuclear weapons programmes operate inside this specialised legal framework. Facilities such as [Los Alamos]({{ 'los-alamos/' | relative_url }}), Lawrence Livermore and Sandia are not simply managing ordinary classified projects; they routinely handle information governed by the Atomic Energy Act.
