## Introduction

The link between Ning Li and Eugene Podkletnov is one of the main reasons [superconductors]({{ 'superconductors/' | relative_url }}) became associated with antigravity research during the 1990s. Neither scientist demonstrated a practical antigravity machine, yet both became central figures in a wave of claims suggesting that specially configured superconductors might interact with gravity in unexpected ways. Their work was often discussed together because both focused on rotating high-temperature superconductors, both attracted attention from aerospace and defence circles, and both appeared to offer possible experimental routes toward [gravity modification]({{ 'gravity-leap/' | relative_url }}). The crucial distinction is that Li primarily advanced a theoretical mechanism, whereas Podkletnov claimed to have observed a measurable gravity-related effect in the laboratory. The continuing gap between theory, claim, and independent replication became one of the defining features of the entire field. ## Why Superconducting Gravity Claims Spread So Fast

The 1990s were a period of unusual optimism about breakthrough propulsion concepts. High-temperature superconductors had been discovered only a few years earlier, and researchers were still exploring their possible implications. At the same time, NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics programme and other speculative research initiatives were investigating whether established physics contained overlooked routes to revolutionary spaceflight technologies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4079954_Prospects_for_Breakthrough_Propulsion_from_Physics" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Prospects for Breakthrough Propulsion from PhysicsThis Project has three grand challenges: (1) Discover propulsion that...</span></span></span>

Into this environment came two ideas that appeared to reinforce one another:

* [Podkletnov]({{ 'podkletnov/' | relative_url }}) reported weight-reduction effects above rotating superconducting discs.
* Li proposed theoretical models suggesting that aligned quantum states in superconductors might generate gravitomagnetic effects under certain conditions.

Although these ideas were not identical, they were easily grouped together by journalists, enthusiasts and some researchers as evidence that superconductors might offer a path toward gravity control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992PhyC..203..441P/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ui.adsabs.harvard.edu">[harvard.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ui.adsabs.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ADSA possibility of gravitational force shielding by bulk YBa 2...by E Podkletnov · 1992 · Cited by 214 — The sample was found to lose f...</span></span></span>

The result was a feedback loop. Podkletnov appeared to offer experimental evidence, while Li appeared to offer a theoretical framework that might explain such evidence. Together they created a narrative that seemed more compelling than either line of work alone.

## Li's Theoretical Route Versus Podkletnov's Shielding Claim

### Ning Li's approach

Li's published work did not begin with a claim that gravity had already been altered. Instead, she explored whether superconducting materials could create unusually strong gravitomagnetic fields through coherent quantum behaviour. In simplified terms, her models suggested that large numbers of paired electrons inside a superconductor might collectively produce effects much stronger than conventional calculations would predict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.technologytoday.us/PastColumnPDFs/30_Anti-Gravity.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: technologytoday.us">[technologytoday.us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">technologytoday.us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Building an Anti-gravity Machine - Technology TodayNing Li theorized that a superconductor rotat- ing very fast, in a very strong magneti...</span></span></span>

Her research therefore occupied a speculative but recognisably theoretical position. The challenge was always experimental [verification]({{ 'verification/' | relative_url }}). The NASA–University of Alabama in Huntsville effort ultimately struggled to build instrumentation capable of measuring the tiny effects under investigation, and the most ambitious rotating-disc experiments were not completed as originally envisioned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/170046/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[Defense Intelligence Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File Id</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">failed to see expected shielding effect in a Podkletnov-like experiment (Reference 29).Read more...</span></span></span>

### Podkletnov's approach

Podkletnov took the opposite path. In a 1992 paper, he reported that objects positioned above a levitated rotating YBCO superconducting disc appeared to lose between roughly 0.05% and 0.3% of their weight. Later accounts described larger claimed effects under more complex operating conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992PhyC..203..441P/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ui.adsabs.harvard.edu">[harvard.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ui.adsabs.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ADSA possibility of gravitational force shielding by bulk YBa 2...by E Podkletnov · 1992 · Cited by 214 — The sample was found to lose f...</span></span></span>

His claim became known as "gravity shielding" because he suggested that the superconducting system somehow reduced the influence of Earth's gravitational field above the apparatus. If correct, such an effect would have represented a profound challenge to established gravitational physics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Podkletnov" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Eugene Podkletnov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eugene Podkletnov</span></span></span>

The attraction of Podkletnov's work was obvious: unlike a mathematical proposal, it appeared to offer an observable laboratory phenomenon. The weakness was equally obvious: other groups struggled to reproduce it.

### Why the two became linked

Li never simply endorsed Podkletnov's interpretation, nor did Podkletnov derive his claims directly from Li's theories. Yet both became associated because they shared three characteristics:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. High-temperature superconductors were central to both approaches.
2. Rotating superconducting systems appeared prominently in both discussions.
3. Both suggested effects dramatically larger than conventional gravitational theory predicted.

</div>

For supporters, Li supplied a possible mechanism and Podkletnov supplied apparent evidence. For critics, both represented examples of extraordinary claims that lacked extraordinary proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19990046249/downloads/19990046249.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Podkletnov has reported that a gravity shielding effect was seen above a YBCO superconductor being rotated by a magnetic field and irradi...</span></span></span>

## Why Rotating Superconductors Attracted NASA and Defence Attention

The attraction was not primarily UFO speculation. From the perspective of [aerospace]({{ 'aerospace/' | relative_url }}) research, even a tiny reproducible gravity-related effect would have been revolutionary.

A confirmed gravity-shielding or gravitomagnetic amplification phenomenon could potentially affect:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* spacecraft propulsion,
* inertial control,
* launch systems,
* satellite station-keeping,
* deep-space travel concepts.

</div>

Because the potential payoff was enormous, agencies could justify investigating claims even when the probability of success appeared low. NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics programme specifically examined unconventional concepts that, if real, would transform spaceflight. Podkletnov's claims were considered important enough to evaluate, and Li's work was considered important enough to receive research support and experimental development funding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4079954_Prospects_for_Breakthrough_Propulsion_from_Physics" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Prospects for Breakthrough Propulsion from PhysicsThis Project has three grand challenges: (1) Discover propulsion that...</span></span></span>

A later [historical review]({{ 'historical-review/' | relative_url }}) prepared for the US defence community noted that Li and colleagues attempted experiments resembling aspects of the Podkletnov approach but did not observe the expected shielding effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/170046/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[Defense Intelligence Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File Id</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">failed to see expected shielding effect in a Podkletnov-like experiment (Reference 29).Read more...</span></span></span>

This detail is important because it demonstrates that institutional interest did not imply institutional validation. Agencies investigated the claims precisely because they were unproven.

## How Replication Problems Shaped the Evidence Gap

The central problem connecting Li and Podkletnov is not secrecy but replication.

Podkletnov's reported weight-loss effects generated widespread interest, yet independent confirmation never emerged at a level capable of persuading the broader physics community. NASA-related reviews, propulsion assessments and later surveys consistently identified gravity-shielding claims as unconfirmed. Experimental programmes found either null results or results that could not be reliably separated from mundane forces, vibration effects, magnetic interactions or measurement artefacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Propulsion_Physics_Project" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Project</span></span></span>

Her theoretical proposals inspired experimental efforts, but publicly documented results never established the large gravity modifications that enthusiasts expected. The experimental platform remained incomplete, and the predicted effects never became part of accepted physics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19990046249/downloads/19990046249.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Podkletnov has reported that a gravity shielding effect was seen above a YBCO superconductor being rotated by a magnetic field and irradi...</span></span></span>

The result was an unusual evidentiary landscape:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* There were real scientists working on real superconductors.
* There were published theories and experimental claims.
* There was genuine government and aerospace interest.
* There was no broadly accepted, independently replicated demonstration of gravity control.

That combination allowed speculation to flourish. Supporters interpreted the absence of confirmation as evidence of suppression or secrecy, while critics viewed the same absence as evidence that the effects were not real.

## What the Li–Podkletnov Comparison Reveals

Comparing Li and Podkletnov helps explain why superconducting antigravity stories became so influential within UFO and advanced-propulsion circles. Their work occupied complementary positions in the same narrative: one researcher appeared to offer a mechanism, the other appeared to offer an experimental result.

Yet the historical record shows a more complicated picture. Podkletnov's gravity-shielding observations remained controversial and largely unreplicated. Li's gravitomagnetic theories attracted serious interest but never produced publicly verified antigravity technology. NASA, defence researchers and propulsion specialists examined these ideas because the implications would have been extraordinary if confirmed, not because confirmation had already occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Propulsion_Physics_Project" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Project</span></span></span>
